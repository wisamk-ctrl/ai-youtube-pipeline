# Critic rubric — English version (phase translate)

The English episode is a translation of the approved Arabic episode: same stories, same sources,
same shot plan. This rubric scores it as a listener HEARS it. Defects are counted, not felt:

    penalty = 2 × (major defects) + 1 × (minor defects)
    score   = 10 − 2 × penalty ÷ (number of segments), rounded, floor 1

**Major** = a listener would be confused, misled, or would stop listening.
**Minor** = a listener notices a wobble but keeps following.
Quote the exact words of every defect. A defect without a quote does not count. Something this
rubric does not name is taste, not a defect. Production markers in [square brackets] are not spoken.

The audience is three groups at once: enterprise leaders, a broad audience curious about AI, and
IT people / techies. The scores use the same six keys as the Arabic critic; here the key `arabic`
means **the English itself**.

## hook — the first 20 seconds (before the greeting)
- MAJOR: the first sentence is a greeting, a date or the channel name, not a news fact.
- MINOR: the hook carries more than one story, one number or one question, or exceeds 45 words.

## clarity — clear for the ear, on first hearing
- MAJOR: a sentence carrying three or more figures.
- MAJOR: a pronoun whose referent could be two different things.
- MINOR: a sentence over 25 words, or a technical term not explained in the same sentence.
- MINOR: written-English constructions that do not work aloud (long parentheticals, "the former/the latter", abbreviations like "e.g.").

## pacing — nothing drags or rushes
- MAJOR: more than 60 spoken words with no [Pause].
- MINOR: three consecutive sentences over 20 words.

## flow — signposts and transitions
- MINOR: a new story starts with no spoken signpost.
- MINOR: a transition that reads as translated ("And now we go to…", calques from Arabic).

## arabic — here: natural spoken English
- MAJOR: a sentence that reads as a word-for-word translation and would sound odd from a native speaker.
- MINOR: hype or filler ("game-changer", "revolutionary", "mind-blowing", "insane") — the show informs, it does not sell.
- MINOR: an Arabic word or Arabic-script name left in the English.

## value — sourced, informational, never advice
- MAJOR: an imperative or recommendation about investing, buying, selling, legal or tax matters ("you should buy", "invest now").
- MAJOR: a figure or claim stated with no attribution where the Arabic script attributed it ("according to …").
- MINOR: a story that never says why it matters to businesses or to the region.
