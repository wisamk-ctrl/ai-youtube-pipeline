# Lessons from past episodes (read with the style guide; every writer, reviewer and critic pass sees this)

Each lesson comes from a real fault heard or caught in a past episode. Treat a repeat as a MAJOR defect.

1. **A name is said once.** W45 voiced «أوبن إيه آي OpenAI» as two names, eleven times. Write the Arabic
   spoken form with the Latin in braces: «أوبن إيه آي{OpenAI}». Never Arabic + bare Latin.
2. **No English in brackets.** «(AI agents)», «(on-behalf-of)», «(sovereign AI)» were all read aloud in W45.
   If a term needs explaining, explain it in Ammani words or put it on screen.
3. **Letters-and-digits names need a spoken form.** G42 was read «جي اتنان وأربعون»; write «جي فورتي تو».
   Claude was read "cloud": write «كلود». HUMAIN was spelled letter by letter: write «هيوماين».
4. **No vowel marks, and no ambiguous words.** «عن بُعد» was read «عم بوعد», and without marks «بعد» reads
   as "after". Say «من بعيد».
5. **Never say anything about this channel's own tools or lists unless it is checked.** W45 said Tencent was
   not on our watchlist; it was (as "Tencent Hunyuan"). The radar is a lead, not a source: cite the reports
   behind it, never «الرادار» as evidence, and make no claim about what we do or do not track.
6. **One product name per idea.** «صلاحيات IAM… PrivateLink… CloudTrail» in one sentence is a list of
   foreign sounds to the ear. Name the one that matters; describe the rest.
7. **Version numbers are not spoken.** «2.1.317» goes on screen; say «آخر إصدار».
8. **One figure per breath.** «عشرة فاصلة تمانية ثانية مقابل تمانية وتمانين فاصلة أربعة» lost listeners;
   give the comparison in words («أسرع بحوالي تمن مرات») and put both numbers on screen.
9. **Vendor figures are the vendor's.** Six NVIDIA numbers in a row read as an advert. Say whose number it is
   in the same sentence, and keep to four vendor figures a segment.
10. **One story in the cold open.** W45 opened with three headlines in 20 seconds. One story, one number,
    one question to the viewer, then the promise of what's coming.
11. **Vary «ليش بيهمّنا».** W45 used it seven times. At most three, in different words.
12. **The region is the edge.** Regional stories (TII, BAREC, SDAIA) got one line each while vendor blogs got
    minutes. Lead with what changes something for people here.
13. **Formal Arabic leaks in.** «بالمقابل», «مقارنة بـ», «الماء», «بشكل متوافق مع» were flagged in W45.
    Use the Ammani table in the style guide.

- **Explain every unfamiliar name the moment it appears (first viewers, 2026-11-15).** A technical friend could not
  follow «Rubin» or «Daytona». Any product, company or term a non-specialist would not know gets a few plain words
  right there: «Daytona, a company that runs code sandboxes for AI agents». One short clause, not a lecture; names
  everyone knows (OpenAI, Google, NVIDIA) need none. If a story needs more than three such names, drop the least
  important ones instead of explaining them all.

- **The first minute decides the episode (YouTube retention, W45-en, 2026-11-19).** Half the viewers had left by
  0:55 and the steepest drop came at 0:11, inside the opening. So: the first words are the lead story and why it
  matters to someone here, said in the first 20 seconds; no greeting, no channel introduction and no list of
  what's coming before it. The welcome and "this week on AI This Week" come after the first story, in one
  sentence. Never open with the show's name, the date or "in this episode". The Monday report's "Where viewers
  leave" section checks whether this works; revise this lesson from those numbers.

- **Arabic voice: English stays English (founder voice test, 2026-11-19).** The Arabic voice read Arabised
  names and spelled-out numbers badly. Write company, product and tech names in Latin letters as people say
  them in Amman offices (OpenAI, ADNOC, G42, NVIDIA, AI agents, CPU, CTO, data sovereignty), and figures as
  digits (5 سنين، 2.6 مليار دولار). Names the voice still gets wrong go in config/pronunciation.yaml as a
  spoken spelling (ADNOC → Adnok, HUMAIN → Humane, G42 → G forty-two); captions keep the real name.

- **Thumbnail words: a plain fact of scale, not a metaphor (YouTube's own suggestions on W47-en, founder
  2026-11-23).** W47's thumbnail said "Microsoft Goes Dark". YouTube's suggested wordings for the same episode
  were "Multi-Day Outage", "Days Offline", "Record IPO": each names how long, how big or a record, which makes a
  viewer ask how that happened, and each reads in under half a second on a phone. So: two words where two will
  do; a duration, a size or a record that the script actually states; no metaphor, no figure that is not in the
  script. When the episode has two big stories the title already names, the second thumbnail (B) takes the
  second story in the same style. The colours and layout stay as they are (founder).
