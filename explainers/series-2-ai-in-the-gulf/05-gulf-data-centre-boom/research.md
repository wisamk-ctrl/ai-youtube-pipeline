# Episode 05: The Gulf's AI data centre boom (research)

Researched 2026-11-26. Source ids refer to `items.json` (prefix `05-gulf-data-centre-boom-`). Every fact below comes from a page fetched for this episode.

**Editorial note:** this year's story is shaped by the 2026 Iran war. AWS facilities in the UAE and Bahrain were hit in March, and the UAE's flagship campus is reportedly being redesigned. Report these events factually and attribute them, without taking a side. Several 2026 delivery dates (Stargate UAE's first 200MW, HUMAIN's Q2 2026 sites, the AWS Saudi region) had not been confirmed as met when this was written. Phrase them as "planned" or "announced", never "live".

## Facts

### What a megawatt means
1. The IEA says traditional data centres use between 10 and 25 MW. Hyperscale AI centres can exceed 100 MW, equivalent to the annual electricity consumption of 100,000 households [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-25].
2. The largest data centre under construction could consume as much electricity as 2 million households, according to the IEA [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-25].
3. Knight Frank defines a data-centre megawatt as one million watts, usually meaning the IT load or computing power a site can support. A gigawatt is 1,000 megawatts [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-18].
4. Data centres used 1.5% of worldwide electricity in 2025. The IEA's base case sees that rising to about 3% by 2030, around 950 TWh, slightly more than Japan's total consumption today [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-25].

### UAE: Stargate UAE and the UAE–US AI Campus
5. Stargate UAE was announced on 22 May 2025 as the first international deployment of OpenAI's Stargate and the first "OpenAI for Countries" partnership [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-01].
6. Stargate UAE is a 1GW cluster in Abu Dhabi, with 200MW expected to go live in 2026 [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-01][05-gulf-data-centre-boom-02].
7. G42 builds the cluster, and OpenAI and Oracle operate it. NVIDIA supplies Grace Blackwell GB300 systems, Cisco provides security and networking, and SoftBank is a partner [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-02].
8. The cluster sits inside the wider 5GW UAE–U.S. AI Campus, which G42 says spans 10 square miles and will be powered by nuclear, solar and natural gas [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-02].
9. OpenAI says Stargate UAE could serve compute within a 2,000-mile radius, reaching up to half the world's population [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-01].
10. Khazna Data Centers, a G42 company, is building Stargate UAE. In October 2025, G42 said construction of the first 200MW was "well underway" and that all long-lead equipment had been procured [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-03].
11. In January 2026, G42's CEO said that after the first 200MW the plan is "anywhere between 200 to 500MW per quarter" of additional capacity toward 5GW. The chips are mostly Nvidia, with AMD and Cerebras too [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-06].
12. G42 gave security assurances to the US government to get export licences. The US approved chip sales to the UAE and Saudi Arabia in late November 2025, up to 35,000 Nvidia GB300 systems or equivalent for each country's "AI champion" [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-06].
13. UAE AI minister Omar Al Olama said in January 2026 that the project will cost more than $30 billion, 50% above the initial estimate of around $20 billion in spring 2025 [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-07].
14. Mubadala said first-phase construction was expected to be completed in Q3 2026 [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-07].
15. Reuters reported in September 2026, citing six people familiar with the matter, that the UAE is revising the 5GW project. The single campus would likely become a network of data centres across the country, with air defences, some underground construction, blast-resistant concrete and backup power and cooling under consideration [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-08].
16. G42 told Reuters that work is "progressing as planned" and that the project's specifics are "subject to continuous review" [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-08].
17. According to Reuters, in April 2026 Iran's armed forces issued a video naming Stargate UAE as a potential target [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-08].

### UAE: Khazna and the hyperscalers
18. Khazna plans to add more than 1GW of operational capacity over five years from October 2025, including more than 400MW abroad in markets such as Saudi Arabia and Italy [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-04].
19. Khazna is developing hyperscale facilities in Abu Dhabi, Dubai and Ajman [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-04].
20. Microsoft and G42 announced a 200MW UAE expansion delivered through Khazna, expected to start coming online before the end of 2026. It is part of Microsoft's $15.2 billion UAE investment [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-05].
21. Knight Frank (October 2025) says UAE live capacity passed 376MW, with 19.2MW added in 2025, and UAE vacancy is just 2.4%, one of the lowest in EMEA [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-18].
22. Knight Frank also reports a US$544m Microsoft–du hyperscale facility in Dubai, and describes Abu Dhabi's US$13bn Digital Strategy 2025–2027 as mandating sovereign cloud adoption [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-18].

### Saudi Arabia: HUMAIN and the cloud regions
23. HUMAIN was launched in May 2025 as a subsidiary of the Public Investment Fund. It targets 1.9GW of data centre capacity by 2030 and 6.6GW four years later, which its CEO said would cost $77bn at current market rates [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-11].
24. Riyadh will subsidise electricity prices for HUMAIN's facilities in the kingdom [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-11].
25. HUMAIN said its Eastern Province land could host ten 200MW data centres [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-11].
26. HUMAIN has secured 211 plots of land across Saudi Arabia. Its Riyadh and Dammam sites, 100MW each initially, were expected to go live in Q2 2026 [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-12].
27. HUMAIN's announced partners include AWS (a $5bn "AI Zone"), stc (a JV for 1GW), xAI (a 500MW data centre) and AMD with Cisco (a JV for 1GW over five years) [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-12][05-gulf-data-centre-boom-13].
28. The xAI–HUMAIN 500MW project is xAI's first data centre outside the US, and Grok will be deployed in Saudi Arabia [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-13].
29. AWS and HUMAIN announced a "$5 billion-plus" AI Zone in May 2025, on top of Amazon's US$5.3 billion Saudi cloud region [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-14].
30. In August 2026, AWS said its first Saudi region is on track to launch in December 2026. With HUMAIN it will make up to 50MW available in the AI Zone by 2028 [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-15].
31. Microsoft said its Saudi Arabia East region (Eastern Province, three availability zones) would be available in November 2026. Microsoft says it offers "local data residency" and "low-latency access" [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-16].
32. A release quoting PwC says AI will contribute $130 billion to Saudi Arabia's economy by 2030, more than 40% of an estimated $320 billion for the Middle East [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-14].

### Qatar, Oman, Kuwait, Bahrain
33. Qatar's Qai, a QIA subsidiary launched on 8 December 2025, formed a $20 billion AI infrastructure joint venture with Brookfield [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-19].
34. In November 2026, Syntys, carved out of Ooredoo, broke ground on a 20MW Tier III data centre in Qatar built for higher-density GPU racks. It now owns four campuses in the country [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-20].
35. Syntys operates the facilities that host Ooredoo's sovereign AI cloud, which runs on NVIDIA Hopper GPUs [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-20].
36. A consortium led by Mubbadrah plans an AI data centre of up to 400MW in Salalah, Oman, with $3 billion of initial investment and 400MW of solar with batteries from O2 Global Energy. Crusoe has shown interest as a potential customer [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-21].
37. Kuwait invited bids for three substations, estimated at more than $71.5 million, to power Google Cloud and Microsoft data centres. The project builds on a 2023 Google Cloud region agreement and a 2025 Microsoft Azure partnership [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-22].
38. AGBI reports that Kuwait cut power during peak demand in 2025 and has imported electricity via the GCC Interconnection Authority since March 2025 [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-22].
39. AWS opened its Bahrain region in 2019 and its UAE region in 2022 [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-10].

### Physical risk: the 2026 strikes
40. In March 2026, AWS said two of its UAE facilities were "directly struck" by drones and that a strike close to a Bahrain facility caused physical impacts. Fires set off sprinklers, which damaged equipment [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-09].
41. AWS advised customers to back up data and consider moving workloads to other AWS regions [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-09].
42. In September 2026, AWS said it cannot restore data hosted only in one UAE availability zone (mec1-az2). In Bahrain the damage "exceeded what our regional and multi-AZ services are designed to withstand" [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-10].

### Market size
43. JLL (August 2026) puts Middle East live capacity at 1.6GW, with 2.6GW in development and 13.8GW planned, still pointing to a quadrupling by 2030 [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-17].
44. JLL says regional conflict "stalled major completions over the past six months" and that schemes are being "deferred rather than dropped" [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-17].
45. JLL says AI training needs of 100MW and above are not latency-sensitive and "follow available power", while latency-bound workloads stay near major cities [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-17].

### Heat and water
46. Rest of World and Climate Central found that every data centre in the UAE is in an area averaging above 27°C a year, the top of ASHRAE's recommended range of 18–27°C. Nearly all Saudi data centres are in regions that are too hot [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-23].
47. Citing Mordor Intelligence, Rest of World says Middle East and Africa data centre water use rises from 119 billion litres in 2025 to over 426 billion litres by 2030. The UAE alone is expected to use 61 billion litres a year by 2030 [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-24].
48. Gulf summers push operators toward evaporation-based cooling, and Khazna says it is exploring reuse of treated sewage effluent [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-24].

## Conflicts and dates
- **UAE campus size.** G42 (May 2025) and Reuters (Sept 2026) say 10 square miles, about 26 sq km [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-02][05-gulf-data-centre-boom-08]. The National and AGBI (January 2026) say 19.2 sq km and call it the "5-gigawatt Stargate UAE" [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-07]. That label merges the 1GW Stargate cluster with the 5GW campus. Use G42's split (1GW Stargate inside a 5GW campus) and say the campus is "about 10 square miles".
- **Cost.** About $20bn (spring 2025 estimate) became more than $30bn (Al Olama, January 2026) [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-07]. Reuters calls the 1GW first phase "$30-billion" [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-08]. It is unclear whether $30bn covers the 1GW cluster or more.
- **Stargate UAE's first 200MW.** It was "expected in 2026" (May 2025) and Mubadala pointed to Q3 2026 [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-07]. As of today no fetched source confirms it is operating. A secondary tracker (mwfinder.com, not used as a source) said Khazna pointed to Q4. Re-check before recording.
- **HUMAIN targets.** It cites 1.9GW by 2030 and 6.6GW by 2034 (May 2025) [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-11], while DCD (March 2026) says "approximately 6GW over the next decade" [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-12]. Use 6.6GW by 2034 as the company's stated target. Whether the Q2 2026 Riyadh and Dammam sites went live is unconfirmed.
- **AWS Saudi region.** It was "available in 2026" (May 2025) [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-14] and later "on track for December 2026" (Aug 2026) [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-15]. Not confirmed live as of 26 Nov 2026.
- **Microsoft Saudi Arabia East.** Due in November 2026 [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-16]. Launch not confirmed in a fetched source.
- **Middle East capacity.** JLL H1 2026 puts live capacity at 1.6GW [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-17]. A JLL year-end 2025 snippet (not fetched in full) said 1GW across nine metros, 2.2GW under construction and 12GW planned. These figures move every six months, so always give the date.
- **UAE live capacity.** 376MW (Knight Frank, October 2025) [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-18] is UAE-only and covers a different scope from JLL's regional figure. Don't compare them directly.
- **AWS Gulf status.** It changes often. The latest fetched update is September 2026 [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-10], and AWS promised a Bahrain update in early 2027.

## Proposed outline
- **Working title:** "Gulf AI Data Centres Explained: Gigawatts, Heat and War" (54 characters)
- **First 15 seconds:** "The Gulf has promised to build more AI computing than almost anywhere outside the US and China. Here's what a 'gigawatt' of AI actually is, who's building it, and what 2026 taught everyone about the risks."

**Chapter 1: What a gigawatt of AI means** (facts 1–4, 9)
Data centres are measured in power, not floor space. Cover IEA scale (10–25MW traditional, 100MW+ hyperscale AI, about 100,000 homes), MW and GW definitions, and why distance matters for some workloads but not for AI training.

**Chapter 2: The UAE bet: Stargate, Khazna, Microsoft** (facts 5–14, 18–22)
Cover the 1GW cluster inside a 5GW campus, who does what, the $30bn price, Khazna's 1GW plan, Microsoft's 200MW, and the 2.4% vacancy rate.

**Chapter 3: Saudi Arabia's HUMAIN and the rest of the Gulf** (facts 23–39)
HUMAIN targets 1.9GW by 2030 and 6.6GW by 2034, with land plots, partners (AWS, xAI, stc, AMD/Cisco) and subsidised power. Then Qatar (Qai–Brookfield, Syntys), Oman (400MW Salalah), Kuwait's grid constraint, and Bahrain as an early AWS hub.

**Chapter 4: Heat, water and missiles** (facts 40–48, 15–17)
Covers running above 27°C, water projections, evaporative cooling versus wastewater reuse, the AWS strikes, data loss, the reported redesign into hardened and distributed sites, and JLL's "deferred, not dropped".

**Chapter 5: What it means for a business in the region** (facts 30, 31, 41, 43–45)
Local cloud regions bring data residency and lower latency. The AWS lesson is to design for multi-region and backups, since "in-country" does not mean "single site". Market outlook: quadrupling by 2030, per JLL.

- **Analogy for the hardest idea (MW vs compute):** a data centre's megawatts are like a restaurant's kitchen gas line, not its seats. The size of the pipe decides how much cooking can happen at once. A 100MW AI site has a "gas line" as big as 100,000 homes' electricity, per the IEA.
- **On-screen visuals:**
  - Gulf map with project pins, sized by MW and labelled "announced", "under construction" or "operating".
  - A nested-squares graphic: 200MW inside 1GW inside 5GW.
  - A bar chart of JLL's live, in-development and planned capacity (1.6 / 2.6 / 13.8 GW, H1 2026).
  - A thermometer graphic with ASHRAE's 18–27°C band against Gulf average temperatures.
  - An "availability zone" explainer diagram: three zones, one lost, then two lost.
  - A partner table: who builds, who operates, who supplies chips.
- **Closing "what to watch next":** "When the first 200 megawatts of Stargate UAE, and HUMAIN's first sites, are confirmed live, will they be the hardened, spread-out design the war pushed for? And will the region still quadruple its capacity by 2030?"

## Shorts
1. **"What is a gigawatt of AI?"** A hyperscale AI data centre can draw over 100MW, the electricity of about 100,000 homes (IEA). The UAE's campus plan is 5GW, 50 times that. Takeaway: AI is now measured in power plants, not buildings [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-25][05-gulf-data-centre-boom-02].
2. **"Too hot for a data centre?"** ASHRAE recommends 18–27°C, and every data centre in the UAE sits in an area averaging above 27°C (Rest of World / Climate Central). Takeaway: in the Gulf, cooling and water are part of the AI bill [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-23].
3. **"When the cloud got hit."** In March 2026, drones struck AWS facilities in the UAE and Bahrain, and AWS later said some data hosted in a single zone could not be restored. Takeaway: "in-country" isn't a backup plan, so spread your workloads [05-gulf-data-centre-boom-09][05-gulf-data-centre-boom-10].
