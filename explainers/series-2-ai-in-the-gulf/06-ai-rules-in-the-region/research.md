# Research: AI rules in the Gulf: what businesses need to know

Episode 06, series 2 ("AI in the Gulf"). Researched 2026-11-26. Sources: `items.json` (25 sources, ids `06-ai-rules-in-the-region-01` to `-25`; below, `[NN]` is short for `[06-ai-rules-in-the-region-NN]`).

> **Informational only, not legal advice.** This episode explains what published laws, regulations and guidance say, in plain business terms. It is not legal advice, and it does not tell any business what to do. Rules change often (see "Conflicts and dates"). Anyone making a compliance decision should check the current official text and talk to a qualified lawyer in the relevant jurisdiction. The script should say this on air at the start and at the end.

## Facts

### The big picture
1. The UAE alone has three data protection regimes: the federal (onshore) one, the DIFC's and the ADGM's. With Saudi Arabia that makes four separate regimes, each with its own law and regulator [04].
2. HFW lists specific personal data protection laws in force in Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Qatar, the Qatar Financial Centre, the UAE, ADGM and DIFC, and warns that a "one size fits all" GCC strategy raises the risk of non-compliance in a particular jurisdiction [24].
3. On the most basic question, what lawful basis lets you process personal data without consent, the four UAE/Saudi regimes give three different answers: GDPR-style legitimate interests in DIFC and ADGM, ten listed exceptions and no legitimate interests basis onshore UAE, and a narrow legitimate interests basis in Saudi Arabia [04][24].
4. Of the four UAE/Saudi regimes, only the DIFC has AI-specific data protection provisions. The other three reach AI only indirectly, through impact assessments for new technology and rules on decisions made solely by automated means [04].

### UAE onshore: the federal PDPL
5. The federal personal data law was issued on 20 Sep 2021, published in the Official Gazette on 26 Sep 2021 and took effect on 2 Jan 2022. The official portal lists it as "Active" [01].
6. The UAE PDPL is Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021. Its regulator, the UAE Data Office, was set up by a separate law, Federal Decree-Law No. 44 of 2021 [02].
7. The PDPL asked for Executive Regulations within six months (Article 28). Once those regulations are issued, companies get no more than six months to comply, which the Cabinet can extend (Article 29) [03].
8. As of 19 November 2026 the Executive Regulations were still not on the official UAE Legislation portal [03]. ABS Partners (28 Oct 2026) also says they are "yet to be issued" and found no published enforcement decision under the PDPL [02]. When we fetched it on 26 Nov 2026, the portal's own page still said the law's last update was on 20 Sep 2021 [01].
9. The PDPL does not set fines itself. Article 26 leaves the violations and penalties to a Cabinet decision, so any mainland fine figure should be treated with care unless it names that decision [03][02].
10. The PDPL does not cover government data, health and banking or credit data that have their own laws, or companies in free zones with their own data protection law, such as DIFC and ADGM [02][03].
11. What the PDPL already requires, in plain terms: consent unless an exception applies, a record of processing, security measures such as encryption, breach notification to the regulator, a Data Protection Officer for high-risk processing (including profiling or automated processing of sensitive data), impact assessments before certain high-risk processing, and a right to object to automated decisions [02][03].
12. Cross-border transfers under the PDPL: data can go to jurisdictions the regulator approves, or on listed grounds such as a contract binding the recipient, explicit consent or contract performance. No adequacy list and no standard contractual clauses have been published, so Stephenson Harwood says businesses transferring from mainland UAE are "operating without official guidance" [04][02].
13. Moving data between mainland UAE and DIFC or ADGM counts as a cross-border transfer [02]. The DIFC's law defines a third country as "a jurisdiction other than the DIFC, whether in the UAE or elsewhere" [04].
14. Health data has a residency rule: under Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 it is stored and processed inside the UAE, and can go outside only if the health authority issues a resolution allowing it [03][02].

### UAE: the new regulator
15. On 14 June 2026 Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid approved a new Artificial Intelligence and Data Authority. He called it "the single national body responsible for data, artificial intelligence and digital government in the UAE, reporting directly to the Cabinet" [05].
16. The Authority takes over functions from three bodies: the Office of Artificial Intelligence, Digital Economy and Remote Work Applications; the Digital Government Sector at the TDRA; and the UAE Data Office. Omar Sultan Al Olama chairs it [05].
17. Its mandate includes proposing national policies and legislation and setting standards and guidelines for data and AI management [05]. Stephenson Harwood says it remains to be seen whether, and on what timeline, data protection gets priority within this broader mandate [04].

### UAE: AI charter and ethics guidelines (soft law)
18. The UAE Charter for the Development and Use of Artificial Intelligence was issued on 10 June 2024 and has twelve principles, including safety, algorithmic bias, data privacy, transparency, human oversight, governance and accountability, and commitment to treaties and applicable laws [11].
19. The Charter is addressed to the public sector, private companies, consumers, AI developers, researchers and others [11].
20. The UAE AI Office's "AI Ethics: Principles & Guidelines" (Dec 2022) sets eight principles: fair; accountable; transparent; explainable; robust, safe and secure; human centred; sustainable; and privacy preserving. It states "The guidelines are non-binding" [12].
21. Those guidelines apply only to AI systems that make or inform "significant decisions", meaning decisions that could significantly affect individuals or society [12].

### DIFC: Regulation 10, the region's AI-specific rule
22. The DIFC Data Protection Law is DIFC Law No. 5 of 2020. Regulation 10 of the DIFC Data Protection Regulations, enacted on 1 September 2023, covers personal data processed through "autonomous and semi-autonomous systems", i.e. AI [06][07].
23. Regulation 10 has been in force since September 2023 and moved to full enforcement from January 2026 [04].
24. It splits roles the way data law splits controllers and processors. A "Deployer" (who directs or benefits from the system) is like a controller, an "Operator" (who runs it for the Deployer) is like a processor, and a "Provider" develops or sells the system [07].
25. Deployers and Operators must give users clear and explicit notice when they first use a system that processes personal data, including how it affects their rights to correct, erase or object [07]. Regulation 10 also requires a register of AI processing activities [07].
26. Systems used for high-risk processing may not be made available commercially unless they meet the Commissioner's audit and certification requirements. A system's certification lasts at most three years [07]. High-risk processing also requires an "autonomous systems officer", whose role is similar to a DPO's [04][07].
27. Every system must be designed around five concepts (ethical, fair, transparent, secure and accountable) and used for human-defined or human-approved purposes [04].
28. Enforcement is already happening: in a 2025 Regulation 10 thematic assessment, the DIFC Commissioner issued 601 assessments to DIFC entities and received 756 responses [04].
29. The DIFC's broader data enforcement is active: more than 300 inspections and 300 thematic assessments in 2024, rising to nearly 1,300 in 2025 [04].

### ADGM
30. ADGM's regime is the Data Protection Regulations 2021. Its fine ceiling is USD 28 million, including the total for several linked contraventions [08][04].
31. ADGM has published only two enforcement actions: a direction in June 2023 and a fine of $20,000 in May 2024 [04].
32. ADGM requires a record of processing from every entity that processes personal data, and an impact assessment before any new project likely to be high risk. It also publishes its own standard contractual clauses for transfers [09]. ADGM's guidance on impact assessments names AI as a new technology that calls for one [04].

### The financial-centre "data corridor"
33. On 29 January 2026 the QFC, ADGM and DIFC announced that each had added the other two to its adequacy list, so personal data can move between the three centres without extra safeguards [10][04].
34. Neither the DIFC nor the ADGM list includes onshore UAE or Saudi Arabia, so a DIFC firm sending data to its own affiliate in Dubai mainland or Riyadh needs safeguards [04].

### Finance: central banks on AI
35. The Central Bank of the UAE announced its AI guidance note for licensed financial institutions on 23 February 2026. It covers governance and accountability, fairness, transparency and explainability, human oversight, and data and privacy [13]. The rulebook shows it as issued on 11/2/2026 [14].
36. Under the CBUAE note, boards and senior management are accountable for AI outcomes, banks "should not employ AI models that they have no control over", and they should keep an inventory of all AI models with each model's purpose and risk rating [14].
37. The note expects bias testing at least once a year, or whenever a model is upgraded or materially changed. It also expects disclosures in Arabic and English, and lets customers ask for a human to review an AI decision [14].
38. The note says personal data must follow the "rules on data being retained in the country". Banks stay responsible for outsourced AI and must always be able to stop using a model, with human intervention. Fully autonomous "human-out-of-the-loop" use is for low-risk, non-material processes only [14].
39. Qatar Central Bank issued its AI Guideline on 4 September 2024 to regulate the use of AI in the financial sector [20]. The Global Banker reports it applies to all QCB-licensed entities, and that firms need QCB approval before signing agreements for high-risk AI systems and before launching any new AI system [21].

### Saudi Arabia
40. The Saudi PDPL came into effect on 14 September 2023, and businesses generally had until 14 September 2024 to comply. SDAIA is the regulator [15]. It also applies to entities outside the Kingdom that process data of people living there [15].
41. Breaches must be reported to SDAIA within 72 hours [15][04]. Fines go up to SAR 5 million per breach and can double for repeat violations. Disclosing sensitive data with intent to harm can bring up to two years in prison and/or a SAR 3 million fine [15][04].
42. Enforcement is live: SDAIA announced in early 2026 that it issued 48 decisions in 2025 against organisations violating the PDPL. A respondent has five days to answer an alleged violation [04].
43. Saudi transfers need a reason as well as a safeguard: data can leave only for listed purposes, must not harm national security or the Kingdom's vital interests, and because SDAIA has published no adequacy list, every transfer currently goes through safeguards such as standard contractual clauses or binding corporate rules [04][15]. In banking, transfers may also need approval from the Saudi Central Bank [15].
44. SDAIA's AI Ethics Principles (September 2023) set seven principles and four risk levels. Unacceptable-risk systems, such as social profiling, child exploitation or behaviour distortion, are "not allowed", and high-risk systems need conformity assessments before and after deployment [16].
45. SDAIA has published generative AI guidelines for the private sector, non-profits and individuals, including steps to avoid copyright problems [17].
46. In May 2026 SDAIA issued Deepfakes Guidelines. They warn of financial fraud and voice and face impersonation, and call for consent, protection of personal data, digital watermarks and source verification [18].
47. In October 2026 SDAIA put amendments to the PDPL Implementing Regulations out for consultation (closed 5 November, final text pending). One proposal would remove the harm threshold for reporting breaches, so more incidents would need to be reported [04].
48. Saudi Arabia's draft Global AI Hub Law (14 April 2025) proposes "data embassies": data centres in Saudi Arabia where foreign data is held under a foreign country's law [19].

### Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Kuwait
49. Qatar's Law No. 13 of 2016 made it the first GCC state with a general data protection law. Processing sensitive data requires authorisation from the national cyber regulator (NCGAA), and breaches likely to cause damage must be reported within 72 hours [22].
50. Bahrain's law is Law No. 30 of 2018, in force since 1 August 2019. Oman's is Royal Decree 6/2022, in force since 13 February 2023, and in Oman the data subject's express consent is generally needed before personal data leaves the country [24].
51. Kuwait has no single, state-wide data protection law and no dedicated AI law. CITRA's data privacy regulation (Decision No. 26 of 2024) now applies only to licensed telecom and internet providers, who must report breaches within 24 hours [25].

## Conflicts and dates

- **UAE PDPL issue date:** the official portal gives an issued date of 20 Sep 2021 and an Official Gazette date of 26 Sep 2021 [01]. HFW (Oct 2024) says "issued 26 September 2021" [24]. Use the portal: issued 20 Sep, published 26 Sep, effective 2 Jan 2022.
- **UAE PDPL Executive Regulations:** sources from Oct and Nov 2026 say they have not been issued [02][03][04]. PRO TECHnology warns that some web pages claim a regulation number or a 2026 issue date [03]. Check the official portal again on the day of recording; this is the most likely fact to change.
- **Name of the new UAE authority:** WAM calls it the "Artificial Intelligence and Data Authority" [05]. Stephenson Harwood calls it the "Federal AI and Data Authority" [04]. Use the WAM name.
- **Whether the UAE Data Office worked in practice:** Stephenson Harwood says it "had not become fully operational in practice" based on public information [04]. ABS Partners calls it "a regulator established on paper" [02]. Both are law-firm assessments, so attribute them.
- **UAE AI Charter date:** the legislation portal says issued 10 Jun 2024 [11]. The CBUAE note says it was "published in July 2024" [14]. Use June 2024, or just "2024".
- **CBUAE AI guidance note date:** the rulebook shows "Issued on 11/2/2026" [14], and the press release is dated 23 February 2026 [13]. One unverified third-party page says it "applies from 16 September 2025"; this is not used (see Unverified). Safe wording: "published in February 2026".
- **QCB AI Guideline date:** QNA reports it on 4 September 2024 [20]. A law-firm note cited it on 25 Sept 2024, and one search snippet said "26 September 2024". Use 4 September 2024.
- **Qatar PDPPL fines:** DLA Piper says fines of up to "5 million (US$1.4 million)" [22]. PwC says fines of QAR 1,000,000 to QAR 5,000,000 [23]. Neither quotes the article. If used, say "fines of up to 5 million riyals, according to DLA Piper".
- **Qatar regulator:** DLA Piper names the NCGAA of the National Cyber Security Agency [22]. PwC mentions 2020 guidelines from a department of the Ministry of Transport and Communications [23]. Supervision appears to have moved; use the NCGAA, attributed to DLA Piper.
- **SDAIA AI ethics principles:** SDAIA's own document lists seven principles [16]. SPA's 2025 article lists "integrity, fairness, privacy, security, reliability, safety, transparency, interpretability, accountability, responsibility, humanity, and social and environmental benefit" [17], which reads as the same seven principles split differently. Use "seven principles" from the primary document.
- **Saudi grace period:** businesses generally had until 14 September 2024 [15]. IAPP (Sept 2025, fetched but paywalled, so not used as an item) says a two-year grace period was later extended to three years from the law's first announcement.
- **Changes often:** UAE Executive Regulations and penalties; the new UAE authority's data protection role; adequacy lists in DIFC, ADGM and QFC; SDAIA's Implementing Regulation amendments (consultation closed 5 Nov 2026); DIFC Consultation Paper No. 3 of 2026 on Regulation 10 certification and a possible new Regulation 11 [04]; enforcement counts in DIFC and Saudi Arabia.

## Shorts

1. **"Three data laws, one country."** Fact: the UAE has three data protection regimes (federal, DIFC and ADGM), and moving data from the mainland into DIFC or ADGM counts as a cross-border transfer [04][02]. Takeaway: in the UAE, which law applies depends on where your entity and data sit, not just which country you're in.
2. **"The Gulf's first AI-specific data rule."** Fact: DIFC Regulation 10 covers personal data processed by AI. It requires notice to users on first use and a register of AI processing, and it moved to full enforcement from January 2026 [07][04]. Takeaway: if you run AI on personal data inside the DIFC, the rules already apply and the regulator is checking.
3. **"Saudi's 72-hour clock."** Fact: under Saudi Arabia's PDPL, breaches go to SDAIA within 72 hours, fines reach SAR 5 million per breach, and SDAIA reported 48 enforcement decisions in 2025 [15][04]. Takeaway: Saudi data protection is being enforced now, not "coming soon".
