[Pause]

In the UAE, one AI tool can fall under three different data protection laws, depending on which side of a free-zone line your company sits.

[Section: One region, many rulebooks]

First, why there is no single "Gulf AI law."

A data protection regime is a set of rules on personal data, plus a regulator to enforce them.

According to the law firm Stephenson Harwood, those three regimes are the federal mainland, the Dubai International Financial Centre, or DIFC, and Abu Dhabi Global Market, or ADGM.

Add Saudi Arabia, and you have four separate regimes. Each has its own law and its own regulator.

The law firm HFW lists specific data laws in Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Qatar, the Qatar Financial Centre, the UAE, ADGM and DIFC. HFW warns that a "one size fits all" Gulf strategy raises the risk of breaking the rules somewhere.

[Pause]

Here's how different they are. Ask a basic question. When can you use someone's personal data without their consent?

[Pause]

DIFC and ADGM allow "legitimate interests." That means a genuine business reason, balanced against the person's rights. Onshore UAE has no such basis, only a list of ten exceptions. Saudi Arabia allows a narrow version.

[Pause]

Four regimes, three answers.

[Pause]

Now the most important split for AI. Data protection laws are binding. Most AI ethics documents in the region are voluntary, often called "soft law."

[On-Screen Text: Binding laws vs voluntary guidance]

According to Stephenson Harwood, of those four regimes, only the DIFC has data rules written specifically for AI. The others reach AI indirectly. They do it through impact assessments for new technology, and rules on decisions made purely by machines.

[Pause]

And not every state has a general law. According to Chambers, a guide to legal systems, Kuwait has no single data protection law and no dedicated AI law.

[Section: The UAE, three systems in one country]

Next, the UAE mainland, and the rules that are still being finished.

The federal Personal Data Protection Law, or PDPL, is Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021.

[On-Screen Text: UAE PDPL: issued 20 Sep 2021 · in force 2 Jan 2022]

The official portal says it was issued in September 2021 and took effect in January 2022.

[Pause]

But a law like this needs Executive Regulations. Those are the detailed rules on how it works in practice. The PDPL asked for them within six months.

[Pause]

As of 19 November 2026, according to the IT firm PRO TECHnology, they were still not on the official legislation portal. The law firm ABS Partners also says they are "yet to be issued."

Once issued, the law says companies get no more than six months to comply. The Cabinet can extend that.

[Pause]

Fines are pending too. The PDPL leaves penalties to a separate Cabinet decision. So treat any mainland fine figure with care unless it names that decision.

Even so, the law already asks for a lot. Consent, unless an exception applies. A record of what data you process. Security measures, such as encryption. Breach notification to the regulator.

[Pause]

It also asks for a Data Protection Officer, the person responsible for compliance, for high-risk processing. It requires impact assessments, meaning a written risk check, before certain high-risk processing. And people get a right to object to automated decisions.

[Pause]

It doesn't cover everything. Government data is out. So are health and banking data with their own laws, and free-zone companies like those in DIFC and ADGM.

[Pause]

Health data has its own residency rule. Under a 2019 federal law, it is stored and processed inside the UAE. It can leave only if the health authority allows it.

[Pause]

Then there's the regulator.

[On-Screen Text: Artificial Intelligence and Data Authority — approved 14 June 2026]

In June 2026, Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid approved a new Artificial Intelligence and Data Authority. He called it "the single national body" for data, AI and digital government, reporting directly to the Cabinet.

It takes over functions from three bodies, including the UAE Data Office. Omar Sultan Al Olama chairs it.

[Pause]

Its mandate includes proposing policies and laws, and setting standards for data and AI. Stephenson Harwood says it remains to be seen whether data protection gets priority within that wider mandate.

[Pause]

Finally, soft law. The UAE AI Charter was issued in June 2024. It has twelve principles, including safety, algorithmic bias, privacy and human oversight. It is addressed to the public sector, private companies, consumers and developers.

The UAE AI Office's 2022 ethics guidelines set eight principles. They say plainly: "The guidelines are non-binding."

[Pause]

[Section: DIFC and ADGM, where AI rules get specific]

Now to the financial free zones, where AI rules get specific.

[On-Screen Text: DIFC Regulation 10 — AI and personal data]

The DIFC's Regulation 10 was enacted in September 2023. It covers personal data processed by "autonomous and semi-autonomous systems." In plain words, AI.

According to Stephenson Harwood, it moved to full enforcement from January 2026.

[Pause]

It assigns three roles. The Provider builds or sells the system. The Operator runs it. The Deployer directs it or benefits from it.

[On-Screen Text: Provider builds · Operator runs · Deployer benefits]

Deployers and Operators must give users clear notice the first time they use the system. That notice covers how it affects their rights to correct, erase or object.

[Pause]

Firms must also keep a register of AI processing activities.

High-risk systems can't be sold commercially unless they meet the regulator's audit and certification requirements.

[Pause]

Certification lasts at most three years. High-risk processing also needs an "autonomous systems officer," a role similar to a Data Protection Officer.

And the regulator is checking. According to Stephenson Harwood, a 2025 Regulation 10 review issued 601 assessments.

[On-Screen Text: 601 assessments · 756 responses]

[Pause]

It received 756 responses.

Next door, ADGM.

[Pause]

ADGM's fine ceiling is 28 million US dollars.

[On-Screen Text: ADGM fine ceiling: USD 28 million]

But Stephenson Harwood counts only two published enforcement actions.

[Pause]

One was a fine of 20,000 dollars, in May 2024. ADGM's guidance names AI as a new technology that calls for an impact assessment.

[Pause]

Now the hardest idea: moving data across borders. Think of personal data as a passenger with a passport. Some destinations are visa-free. That's the "adequacy list," places a regulator trusts. Everywhere else needs a visa, meaning a contract or safeguard.

[Pause]

[On-Screen Text: QFC · ADGM · DIFC — mutual adequacy, Jan 2026]

In January 2026, the Qatar Financial Centre, ADGM and DIFC put each other on their adequacy lists. Data moves between them without extra paperwork.

But neither the DIFC nor ADGM list includes onshore UAE or Saudi Arabia. And moving data between mainland UAE and DIFC counts as a cross-border transfer. Same city, but a visa is still needed.

[Pause]

Onshore UAE has written visa rules into law. But it has published no visa-free list and no standard contract clauses.

[Section: Saudi Arabia and Qatar]

Now Saudi Arabia, where enforcement is already under way.

[Pause]

The Saudi PDPL took effect in September 2023. Businesses generally had until September 2024 to comply. The regulator is SDAIA, the Saudi Data and AI Authority. The law also reaches companies abroad that process data of people living in the Kingdom.

[On-Screen Text: Saudi PDPL: breach report within 72 hours]

[Pause]

Breaches must reach SDAIA within 72 hours. Fines reach 5 million riyals per breach and can double for repeat violations.

[Pause]

SDAIA announced in early 2026 that it issued 48 decisions in 2025 against violators.

[Pause]

Back to our passport. Saudi Arabia also asks why the passenger is travelling. Data can leave only for listed purposes, and never against national security. With no adequacy list published, every transfer needs a safeguard.

[Pause]

On AI, SDAIA's 2023 ethics principles set seven principles and four risk levels.

[On-Screen Text: SDAIA: four AI risk levels]

The top level, unacceptable risk, includes social profiling and child exploitation. Those are "not allowed." High-risk systems need conformity assessments, meaning formal checks, before and after deployment.

[Pause]

SDAIA has also issued guidance on generative AI, including steps to avoid copyright problems. Its May 2026 Deepfakes Guidelines warn of fraud and voice and face impersonation. They call for consent, digital watermarks and source verification.

[Pause]

The rules keep moving. In October 2026, SDAIA consulted on amendments that could mean more breaches must be reported. And a draft law from April 2025 proposed "data embassies": data centres in Saudi Arabia holding foreign data under another country's law.

[Pause]

Qatar was first. According to the international law firm DLA Piper, its 2016 law made Qatar the first Gulf state with a general data protection law. Breaches likely to cause damage must be reported within 72 hours.

[Pause]

Two quick notes on the neighbours. Bahrain's law dates from 2018. In Oman, according to HFW, people generally must give express consent before their data leaves the country.

[Pause]

[Section: Banks first, the sector rules]

Last, the banks, where AI rules are most detailed.

[On-Screen Text: CBUAE AI guidance note — February 2026]

The Central Bank of the UAE published an AI guidance note in February 2026, for licensed financial institutions.

Boards are accountable for AI outcomes. Banks "should not employ AI models that they have no control over." They should keep an inventory of every model, with its purpose and risk rating.

[Pause]

Models should be bias-tested, meaning checked for unfair outcomes, at least once a year, or after a major change. Disclosures come in Arabic and English. Customers can ask for a human to review an AI decision.

[Pause]

Banks stay responsible for outsourced AI and must always be able to switch a model off. Fully autonomous AI is for low-risk processes only.

Qatar Central Bank issued its own AI Guideline in September 2024.
