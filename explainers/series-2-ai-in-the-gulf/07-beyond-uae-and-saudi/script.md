Qatar's wealth fund has backed a twenty billion dollar AI infrastructure venture. And Qatar is only one of several countries building serious AI plans beyond the UAE and Saudi Arabia. By the end, you'll know what each of them is really betting on.

[Pause 2s]

[Section: Qatar: capital and an Arabic voice]

First stop: Qatar, which writes big cheques and also builds its own Arabic model.

Qatar's national AI strategy started in a research lab. The Qatar Computing Research Institute, or QCRI, at Hamad Bin Khalifa University wrote the blueprint. The government announced it as the national strategy in October 2019.

[Pause]

The ministry called it the first strategy of its kind written jointly by a government and a research institute. It has six pillars, from talent and data access to AI ethics.

I'm Wissam, and this is AI This Week, AI Explained.

[Pause]

According to PwC, Qatar aims to become a leading regional AI hub by 2030. In May 2024, the communications minister pointed to an incentive package from the Amir for digital transformation.

[Pause]

[On-Screen Text: QR 9 billion digital incentive package (Qatar MCIT, May 2024)]

It was worth QR 9 billion. He also cited a billion-dollar fund for regional entrepreneurs.

[Pause]

Then the money got bigger. In December 2025, Brookfield and Qai announced a joint venture for AI infrastructure. Brookfield is an investment firm that runs a global AI infrastructure fund. Qai is the AI company of the Qatar Investment Authority, or QIA. QIA is Qatar's sovereign wealth fund, a state-owned investment fund.

[Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Brookfield + Qai: $20 billion AI infrastructure venture (Dec 2025)]

The venture is worth 20 billion dollars, covering Qatar and some international markets. AI infrastructure means the data centres behind AI. It also means compute, which is simply processing power.

[Pause]

Most of that power comes from GPUs, specialised chips that do the heavy maths of AI. Brookfield's chief executive called it the firm's first AI infrastructure investment in the Middle East.

[Pause]

QIA also buys stakes in AI companies. In September 2025, it joined a funding round in Anthropic, the maker of the Claude models.

[Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Anthropic valuation, per QIA: $183bn (Sep 2025) · $965bn (May 2026)]

QIA says that round valued Anthropic at 183 billion dollars, post-money. Post-money means the company's value including the new cash.

[Pause]

In May 2026, QIA says it joined its third straight Anthropic round. QIA says that round valued Anthropic at 965 billion dollars.

[Pause]

Now the Arabic voice. Fanar is Qatar's Arabic AI platform, built in-house by QCRI with ministry sponsorship. According to QCRI's paper, its first main model has about 9 billion parameters. Parameters are the internal settings a model learns. More parameters usually mean more capability, and more cost.

[Pause]

Fanar learned from about a trillion tokens. Tokens are small chunks of text, like pieces of words. The open version supports Modern Standard Arabic, plus Gulf, Levantine and Egyptian dialects.

[Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Fanar ~9B (Dec 2024) → Fanar 2.0 27B (Dec 2025) → Fanar 3.0 planned Dec 2026]

Fanar 2.0 came in December 2025. It has 27 billion parameters. QCRI's director said Fanar 3.0 was planned for December 2026.

[Pause]

Qatar also rents global capacity. PwC cites Microsoft's 2024 expansion of its Qatar cloud region. As of October 2026, ministry figures cited by PwC showed more than 8,000 active users of Microsoft's Copilot assistant.

[Pause]

So Qatar does two things at once. Picture a country opening a restaurant. Renting a famous chef's kitchen gets you serving fast. That's a giant cloud provider, or a big data-centre partner.

[Pause]

Writing your own recipe book, in your own dialect, takes longer. But the menu tastes like home, and the recipes stay in the family. Qatar is doing both.

[Section: Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman: three playbooks]

Next, three smaller Gulf states, each with a different playbook.

Kuwait chose partnership. In March 2025, Microsoft signed a strategic partnership with Kuwait's government. Microsoft said it intends to build an AI-powered Azure region there. An Azure region is a cluster of Microsoft data centres in one country.

[Pause]

Under the deal, government employees are to get Microsoft 365 Copilot, an AI assistant inside office apps. In November 2026, Microsoft opened a Digital Innovation Center in Kuwait.

[Pause]

Kuwait's wealth fund also looks abroad. In June 2025, DatacenterDynamics, citing Bloomberg, reported a move by the Kuwait Investment Authority. It would join the AI Infrastructure Partnership. That partnership was founded by the investment firms BlackRock and MGX, together with Microsoft.

[Pause]

[On-Screen Text: AI Infrastructure Partnership target: up to $30bn equity + ~$70bn debt (DCD/Bloomberg, Jun 2025)]

That partnership aims to raise up to 30 billion dollars in equity, plus about 70 billion in debt. Kuwait's contribution was not disclosed.

[Pause]

On rules, Kuwait moves more slowly. As of November 2026, the AI Regulation Map tracker says Kuwait's national AI strategy was still a draft. It says Kuwait had no AI-specific law imposing obligations.

[Pause]

But a 2026 ministerial decision made AI services a licensable business activity.

Bahrain went rules first. In July 2025, its Information and eGovernment Authority launched a National Policy for the Use of AI. It also adopted the GCC guiding manual on AI ethics.

[Pause]

The policy has four pillars: legal compliance, adoption, public awareness, and cooperation. It ties AI use to Bahrain's Personal Data Protection Law.

[Pause]

Oman is building national pieces. In September 2024, it launched a national AI programme running to 2026. One goal is a place among the top 50 countries in the Government AI Readiness Index.

[Pause]

In September 2025, Oman announced Maeen, an Omani language model to support government institutions. It also announced an AI studio and a national open-data portal.

[Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Oman AI Special Zone, Seeb: ~104,000 m² (Royal Decree 50/2026)]

In 2026, a royal decree created an AI Special Zone in Seeb, Muscat, of about 104,000 square metres. It is aimed at semiconductors, robotics, AI and startups.

[Section: Egypt and Jordan: talent and delivery]

Now to Egypt and Jordan, where the story is people and delivery.

Egypt's National Council for AI launched the second edition of its national strategy, covering 2025 to 2030. It has six pillars and 21 initiatives. They include national AI models, Arabic datasets and a national AI certification system.

[Pause]

The 2030 targets include more than 30,000 trained AI specialists.

[Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Microsoft pledge: train and certify 100,000 people in AI in Egypt]

At the launch, Microsoft pledged to train and certify 100,000 people in AI in Egypt. Fast Company Middle East reports Egypt aims for an estimated 42.7 billion dollars in AI-related value by 2030.

[Pause]

Then came the model. In February 2026, Egypt launched Karnak, its first nationally developed large language model. A large language model, or LLM, is the AI that reads and writes text. It's the engine inside a chatbot.

[Pause]

Officials said Karnak tops Arabic models in the 30-to-40-billion-parameter class. Its exact size has not been confirmed.

[Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Karnak Chat pilot, Egypt MCIT, 23 August 2026]

In August 2026, the ministry launched a pilot of Karnak Chat. Karnak was built by enhancing open-source models, whose designs are published for anyone to use. It targets Modern Standard Arabic and Egyptian colloquial Arabic.

[Pause]

It can run inside local data centres. And a free version sits on Hugging Face, a public library of AI models.

[Pause]

Jordan's lesson is tracking. Jordan's AI plan started in 2023 and runs to 2027. It has 68 projects.

[Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Jordan: 42 of 68 projects in implementation or complete (~62%, Oct 2026)]

In October 2026, Minister Sami Smeirat said 42 of them had reached implementation or completion. That's about 62 percent. Jordan's 2025 progress report counted 14 projects done. Another 28 were underway.

[Pause]

The projects are practical: the Siraj education platform, electricity load forecasting, farm water efficiency and AI traffic management. Jordan also has Mawdoo3, a company focused on Arabic AI, which owns more than 200 Arabic websites.

[Pause]

In November 2024, Saudi-based Naif Al-Rajhi Investment took an undisclosed strategic stake.

[Section: Türkiye: the industrial-scale plan]

Next, Türkiye, with the biggest targets in this group.

Türkiye published its AI Action Plan in August 2026. It runs to 2030. It has 16 actions under four pillars: Recognise, Benefit, Produce and Govern.

[Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Türkiye 2030 targets: ≥1 GW capacity · ≥$10bn mainly private investment]

It targets at least one gigawatt of AI and data-centre capacity. A gigawatt measures power, here how much electricity those data centres can draw. It also targets at least 10 billion dollars of mainly private investment.

[Pause]

On skills, it aims for 10,000 advanced AI specialists and 100,000 AI application professionals. Minister Kacır said the plan includes AI literacy training for 5 million people.

[Pause]

It creates a national AI research fund of at least 10 billion lira. And a National AI Board, chaired by the President, sits on top.

[Pause]

Türkiye also builds its own models, often with defence firms involved. T3AI, from the T3 Foundation and defence firm Baykar, came out as an open-source beta in July 2025. It drew on 1,792 volunteers.

[Pause]

Kumru, from the startup VNGRS, has 7.4 billion parameters. It runs on a consumer graphics card with 16 gigabytes of memory. That suits regulated sectors that need AI on their own premises. Its early test version went viral for wrong answers.

[Pause]

HAVELSAN, a Turkish defence-technology company, has a platform called MAIN. It contains a 9-billion-parameter Turkish-focused model. And in November 2026, the minister said a first version of BİLGE, a home-grown Turkish model, would soon open to developers.

[Section: Morocco and the scorecard]

Finally, Morocco, and then the scorecard.

[Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Morocco AI 2030: MAD 100bn (~$10bn) added to GDP]

Morocco wants AI to add 100 billion dirhams to GDP by 2030. That's about 10 billion dollars. It also targets 50,000 jobs and 200,000 trained and certified AI talents.

[Pause]

The work runs through a network of AI institutes called JAZARI. The government wants a large local model that understands Darija, Moroccan spoken Arabic, and Amazigh.

[Pause]

Morocco plans close to a gigawatt of data-centre capacity. That includes a planned Dakhla complex of up to 500 megawatts, on renewable energy. Named partners include Mistral AI, Oracle, Nokia and Orange Business.

[Pause]

So, the scorecard. Capital: Qatar and Kuwait use wealth funds to buy into compute. Rules: Bahrain writes policy first. National models: Qatar, Egypt, Oman, Türkiye and Morocco. Skills at scale: Egypt, Türkiye and Morocco. And publishing delivery progress: Jordan.

[Pause]

This next part is my own view. In my years across this region, I've learned that strategies are easy to announce and hard to land. Headline numbers get the attention. The number I'd watch is usage. Jordan counting projects done, and Qatar reporting active users, show the right instinct.

[Pause]

On models, I don't think it's either-or. Renting a global cloud gets you moving quickly. A national model earns its place when it understands how people actually speak, and when sensitive data must stay home. But it only matters if ministries and companies use it every day. So if I sat with a leader in Kuwait, Muscat or Cairo, I'd ask one question. What will actually run on this, and who will measure it? That's my view. I'd like to hear yours.

[Section: The bottom line]

Three things to remember.

One: beyond the UAE and Saudi Arabia, countries take different paths. Some lead with capital, some with rules, some with models or skills.

Two: most mix both sides of the kitchen. They rent global compute, and they build their own models in their own language and dialects.

[Pause]

Three: the plans are big, but many pieces are still announced or planned. The real test is delivery, and whether people use what gets built.
