[On-Screen Text: One chairman. Three seats.]

One man chairs Abu Dhabi's AI council, the AI company G42, and the AI investment company MGX. [Pause] By the end, you'll have a map of the UAE's AI system: who sets the rules, who holds the money, who builds, and where America fits.

[Section: Two layers of government]

First, who sets direction. In the UAE, there are two layers. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Omar Sultan Al Olama | Minister of State for AI | October 2017]

In October 2017, the UAE appointed Omar Sultan Al Olama as Minister of State for Artificial Intelligence. A US Commerce Department release says the UAE was the first country to appoint a federal minister of AI.

I'm Wissam, and this is AI This Week, AI Explained. [Pause]

That same month, the UAE announced its National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence 2031. [Pause] The aim is for the UAE to be one of the world's AI leaders by 2031.

[On-Screen Text: National AI Strategy 2031 | 8 objectives]

The strategy has eight objectives. Among them are using AI across government services, attracting and training talent, and strong governance and regulation. The UAE AI Office develops and runs it. [Pause]

Titles change. In July 2020, the minister's portfolio grew to include the digital economy and remote work. He is also Deputy Managing Director of the Dubai Future Foundation, a link between the federal level and Dubai. [Pause]

Now the second layer: the emirate of Abu Dhabi.

[On-Screen Text: AIATC | Artificial Intelligence and Advanced Technology Council | 22 January 2024]

On 22 January 2024, a law created the Artificial Intelligence and Advanced Technology Council, or AIATC. Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed issued it as Ruler of Abu Dhabi. The council sets policy and strategy for AI research, infrastructure and investment in Abu Dhabi. [Pause]

At its founding, Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed was named Chairman, and Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Vice Chairman. The members were Khaldoon Al Mubarak, Jassem Al Zaabi, Faisal Al Bannai, and G42's chief executive, Peng Xiao.

[Pause] Remember those names. You'll see them again.

[Section: G42, a family of companies]

Next, the builder. G42 is less one company than a family of them. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: G42 | founded 2018, per Fortune]

Fortune reports that G42 was founded in 2018. It describes Sheikh Tahnoon, the UAE's national security adviser, as its chair and controlling shareholder. [Pause]

G42 lists its investors as Mubadala, Silver Lake, Microsoft and the Dalio Family Office. In November 2025, Abu Dhabi's Media Office called Microsoft a "minority shareholder". Microsoft's Brad Smith sits on G42's board.

G42 calls itself a "neocloud", a new kind of cloud provider built specifically to rent out AI computing. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Core42 | cloud and compute]

First, Core42. In October 2023, G42 merged three units into it: G42 Cloud, Inception and Injazat. Core42 offers sovereign cloud, meaning computing kept under national control, for government and regulated industries. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Inception | AI models | Jais]

Inception didn't stay merged. In August 2024, G42 spun it back out to build AI models. Inception has released 20 open-source Jais models, meaning anyone can download and use them. [Pause] They come in eight sizes, counted in parameters, the internal settings a model learns in training. Jais runs from 590 million to 70 billion. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Presight | listed on ADX, March 2023 | raised USD 496M]

Presight is G42's big-data analytics company. It listed on the Abu Dhabi Securities Exchange in March 2023. Presight says investors asked for [Pause] 136 times the shares on offer. The listing raised 496 million dollars. [Pause]

G42 then owned about 68.5 per cent of Presight. That's after it sold a two per cent stake in September 2025. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Khazna | M42 | Space42]

Khazna builds data centres, the warehouses of servers that AI runs on. G42 calls it a subsidiary. Khazna lists G42, MGX and Silver Lake as shareholders. Mubadala says it worked with G42 on Khazna, on M42 in health technology, and on Space42 in geospatial. [Pause]

What this shows: one group now spans cloud, data, health and space, so its choices shape much of the country's AI stack.

[Section: The money and the brains]

Now, who pays, and who does the research. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: MGX | announced 11 March 2024 | founders: Mubadala + G42]

On 11 March 2024, the AIATC announced MGX, a technology investment company. Its founding partners are Mubadala and G42. It focuses on AI infrastructure, semiconductors, and core AI technologies and applications.

Sheikh Tahnoon chairs MGX, and Khaldoon Al Mubarak is vice-chair. [Pause]

Mubadala itself is large. It reports [Pause] 1.2 trillion dirhams in assets under management for 2024. That's a portfolio of 330 billion dollars. [Pause]

Mubadala says MGX has invested in OpenAI, Anthropic and Databricks. MGX also helped set up the AI Infrastructure Partnership, or AIP, in September 2024. Partners include BlackRock, its unit GIP, Microsoft and NVIDIA.

[On-Screen Text: AIP target: USD 30B equity | up to USD 100B with debt]

AIP aims to mobilise 30 billion dollars of equity, and up to 100 billion including debt. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Aligned Data Centers | enterprise value ~USD 40B | 6.4GW+]

Its first deal closed in July 2026. It bought Aligned Data Centers at an enterprise value of [Pause] about 40 billion dollars. Enterprise value is the price of the whole business, including its debt. [Pause] Aligned has 51 campuses, with more than 6.4 gigawatts operational and planned. A gigawatt measures electrical power, and AI data centres are now sized by the power they use. [Pause]

ADQ, Abu Dhabi's other big state investor, plays through electricity. In March 2025 it agreed an equal partnership with Energy Capital Partners. They plan more than 25 billion dollars across 25 gigawatts of power projects for data centres, mostly in the US. [Pause]

Now the brains. The Advanced Technology Research Council, or ATRC, was set up in 2020. Sheikh Khaled chairs it. By February 2024, ATRC had ten research centres. Together they employed 851 researchers. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: TII → Falcon | AI71]

Its applied research arm, the Technology Innovation Institute, built Falcon. Falcon is a large language model, or LLM: software trained on huge amounts of text to understand and write language. TII open-sourced Falcon 40B and 180B. [Pause] In November 2023, ATRC launched AI71 to sell Falcon to businesses, letting clients host it themselves and keep control of their data. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: MBZUAI | est. 2019 | Masdar City]

Then there's MBZUAI, set up in 2019 in Masdar City. It calls itself the world's first graduate-level, research-based AI university. Its first cohort arrived in January 2021. That group had 78 students from 29 countries. Since February 2025, Khaldoon Al Mubarak has chaired its board.

How does this all fit? [Pause] Picture a family-run airline group. The board, the AIATC, sets strategy. The treasury, Mubadala and MGX, buys stakes in airlines and plane makers abroad. The operating airline, G42 with Core42, Khazna and Presight, flies the routes. [Pause] The flight school, MBZUAI, and the engineering workshop, TII, train pilots and design parts. And the same few people sit on every board, so decisions move fast.

[Section: Dubai's lane]

So far, that's mostly Abu Dhabi. Dubai plays a different role. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Dubai Centre for AI | June 2023]

In June 2023, Sheikh Hamdan bin Mohammed, Crown Prince of Dubai, launched the Dubai Centre for Artificial Intelligence. It sits under the Dubai Future Foundation. It aimed to train [Pause] 1,000 government employees from more than 30 entities in generative AI. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Dubai Universal Blueprint for AI | spring 2024]

In spring 2024, he unveiled the Dubai Universal Blueprint for AI, a yearly plan tied to Dubai's D33 economic agenda. The target is 100 billion dirhams a year from the digital economy. It also seeks a 50 per cent rise in productivity. [Pause]

Phase one has practical steps. Every Dubai government entity gets a Chief AI Officer, with candidates assessed by the Dubai Centre for AI. There are incubators for AI and Web3, meaning blockchain-based internet services. There's a Dubai commercial licence for AI, and fast-tracked land for data centres. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Abu Dhabi: compute, capital, research | Dubai: adoption, attraction]

Read together, these sources suggest a division of labour. Dubai concentrates on using AI in government and attracting companies. Abu Dhabi's institutions hold the computing power, the capital and the research.

[Section: The American bargain]

Finally, why Washington is part of this story. [Pause]

Advanced AI chips are subject to US export controls: rules on which countries and companies may receive them. So access comes with conditions.

[On-Screen Text: Microsoft → G42 | USD 1.5B | April 2024]

In April 2024, Microsoft announced a [Pause] 1.5-billion-dollar investment in G42. G42 agreed to run its AI applications on Microsoft's Azure cloud.

The deal sits under an Intergovernmental Assurance Agreement with the US and UAE governments. Microsoft says it covers cybersecurity, export controls, responsible AI and "know your customer" checks on who uses the computing. [Pause] Bloomberg reports G42 had promised to divest from Huawei, clearing the way. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Microsoft UAE investment 2023–2029: USD 15.2B]

Microsoft says it will invest [Pause] 15.2 billion dollars in the UAE. That spending runs from 2023 to 2029. Just over 7.3 billion comes through the end of 2025. More than 7.9 billion follows from 2026. [Pause]

The chips are GPUs, graphics processors that do the heavy maths of AI. Microsoft says earlier licences brought the equivalent of [Pause] 21,500 Nvidia A100s. Licences approved in September 2025 add the equivalent of 60,400 more. [Pause]

According to Bloomberg, the US Commerce Department then approved chip sales to G42 itself. The volume equalled 35,000 Nvidia GB300s, under "rigorous security and reporting requirements". [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: UAE–US AI Campus | 5GW | Stargate UAE 1GW]

In May 2025, a 5-gigawatt UAE-US AI Campus was unveiled in Abu Dhabi. G42 builds it. Its computing is reserved for US hyperscalers, the giant cloud providers, and approved partners. In return, the White House says the UAE committed to back US data centres "at least as large and as powerful" as those in the UAE. [Pause]

Inside the campus sits Stargate UAE, a 1-gigawatt cluster. Khazna builds it, OpenAI and Oracle operate it, and NVIDIA supplies the chips. OpenAI calls it its first "OpenAI for Countries" partnership. Reuters puts the first phase at [Pause] 30 billion dollars. [Pause]

Plans can shift. In September 2026, Reuters reported, citing six people familiar with the matter, that the UAE is revising the campus after the Iran war. [Pause] It would likely become a network of sites, with air defences and some facilities underground. G42 told Reuters work is "progressing as planned" and details are "subject to continuous review".

[On-Screen Text: Wissam's view]

This next part is my view. [DRAFT VIEW: founder to confirm]

After 25 years selling enterprise software across this region, what strikes me is how few doors there are. [Pause] In most countries, you'd map dozens of agencies. Here, a handful of people sit on the council, the fund, the operator and the university. That makes things move fast, and it helps explain why such large deals got done. [Pause] But concentration cuts both ways. When the same names hold every seat, one decision can redirect the whole system. And the American bargain adds a second set of rules on top. [Pause] So if you sell into this market, learn the boards before the org charts. Track who chairs what, because titles change. And read the conditions on chip shipments as closely as the press releases.

[Section: The bottom line]

Three things to remember. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: 1. Two layers]

One: there are two layers. A federal minister and strategy since 2017, and Abu Dhabi's AIATC since 2024, with the same people across many boards. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: 2. Clear roles]

Two: each player has a role. G42 builds and operates. Mubadala and MGX hold the money. ATRC, TII and MBZUAI do the research. Dubai drives adoption. [Pause]

[On-Screen Text: 3. The American bargain]

Three: chips and capital from America arrive with security conditions attached. And the big campus design, Reuters reports, is under review.
