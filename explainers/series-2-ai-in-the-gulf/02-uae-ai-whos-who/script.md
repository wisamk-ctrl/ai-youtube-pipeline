[Pause]

One man chairs Abu Dhabi's AI council, the AI company G42, and the AI investment firm MGX. By the end, you'll have a map of who sets the rules, who holds the money, who builds, and who America is betting on.

[Pause 2s]

[Section: Two layers of government]

First, who sets the rules. There are two layers: a federal one and an Abu Dhabi one.

[On-Screen Text: Omar Sultan Al Olama · Minister of State for AI · October 2017]

In October 2017, the UAE appointed Omar Sultan Al Olama as Minister of State for Artificial Intelligence. A US Commerce Department release says the UAE was the first country to appoint a federal minister of AI.

I'm Wissam, and this is AI This Week, AI Explained.

Also in October 2017, the UAE announced its National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence 2031. The aim is to be one of the world's AI leaders by then.

[On-Screen Text: National AI Strategy 2031 · 8 objectives]

The strategy has [Pause] eight objectives. They include using AI across government services, attracting talent, and strong governance and regulation. The UAE AI Office carries it out.

Al Olama is also Deputy Managing Director of the Dubai Future Foundation. That links the federal layer to Dubai.

Now the second layer, the one with power over money and machines.

[On-Screen Text: AIATC · Artificial Intelligence and Advanced Technology Council · 22 January 2024]

On 22 January 2024, Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed, as Ruler of Abu Dhabi, issued a law creating the Artificial Intelligence and Advanced Technology Council, or AIATC. It sets policy for AI research, infrastructure and investment in Abu Dhabi.

[Pause]

The founding chairman was Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed. The vice chairman was Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed. The members were Khaldoon Khalifa Al Mubarak, Jassem Al Zaabi, Faisal Al Bannai, and G42's chief executive, Peng Xiao.

[Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Tahnoon · Khaldoon Al Mubarak · Peng Xiao]

Remember three of those names: Tahnoon, Khaldoon Al Mubarak and Peng Xiao. They keep coming back.

[Section: G42, a family of companies]

Next, the builder. G42 is less one company than a family of them.

Fortune reports that G42 was founded in 2018 and is named after *The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*. It describes Sheikh Tahnoon, the UAE's national security adviser, as chair and controlling shareholder.

[Pause]

G42 lists its investors as Mubadala, Abu Dhabi's state investment fund; Silver Lake, a US tech investor; Microsoft; and the Dalio Family Office, the investment arm of hedge-fund founder Ray Dalio's family.

In November 2025, the Abu Dhabi Media Office called Microsoft a "minority shareholder". G42's board then included Microsoft's Brad Smith, alongside Tahnoon, Khaldoon Al Mubarak and Peng Xiao.

[Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Core42 · launched 16 October 2023]

The first family member is Core42. G42 launched it in October 2023 by merging three units: G42 Cloud, Inception and Injazat. It sells sovereign cloud, meaning computing kept under national control, to governments and regulated industries.

Inception did not stay inside. In August 2024, G42 spun it back out to build AI models. It released Jais, a family of Arabic-focused models, as open source, free for anyone to download.

[On-Screen Text: Jais · 20 open-source models · 590M to 70B parameters]

There were twenty Jais models. [Pause] The smallest had 590 million parameters; the largest, 70 billion. Parameters are the adjustable numbers inside a model. Roughly, they measure its size.

Core42 also borrows to grow. In 2026, it raised [Pause] 550 million dollars in trade finance from HSBC. That kind of borrowing hands over no ownership.

[Pause]

[On-Screen Text: Presight · ADX listing March 2023 · 136x oversubscribed]

Next is Presight, G42's big-data analytics company. It listed on the Abu Dhabi Securities Exchange in March 2023. Investors asked for [Pause] 136 times more shares than were on offer. The company says it raised 496 million dollars.

[Pause]

In September 2025, G42 sold a 2 per cent stake. It got 100 million dollars for it. According to The National, it kept about 68.5 per cent.

Then there's Khazna, which builds and runs data centres. It lists its shareholders as G42, MGX and Silver Lake. G42 calls it a subsidiary.

[Pause]

[Section: The money and the brains]

Now, who pays, and who does the research.

[On-Screen Text: MGX · announced 11 March 2024]

On 11 March 2024, the AIATC announced MGX, a technology investment company founded with Mubadala and G42. It invests in AI infrastructure, semiconductors, and core AI technology.

Tahnoon chairs MGX. Khaldoon Al Mubarak is vice-chair. The CEO, Ahmed Yahia Al Idrissi, previously ran direct investments at Mubadala.

[On-Screen Text: Mubadala · AED 1.2 trillion AUM (2024)]

Mubadala itself is far bigger. For 2024, it reported [Pause] 1.2 trillion dirhams in assets under management.

[Pause]

Mubadala says MGX invested in OpenAI, Anthropic and Databricks. It also co-led the AI Infrastructure Partnership, or AIP, with partners including Microsoft, BlackRock, NVIDIA and xAI.

AIP aims to mobilise [Pause] 30 billion dollars of equity, and up to 100 billion with debt. Its first deal closed in July 2026. AIP, MGX and GIP, BlackRock's infrastructure fund, bought Aligned Data Centers.

[On-Screen Text: Aligned Data Centers · ~$40bn enterprise value · July 2026]

The enterprise value was about [Pause] 40 billion dollars. That's the whole company's worth, including debt. Aligned had 51 campuses and more than 6.4 gigawatts of capacity, operating and planned. Data centres are sized by the power they draw.

[Pause]

One more investor matters, and it comes in through electricity. ADQ is Abu Dhabi's other big state investor. In March 2025, it agreed an equal partnership with Energy Capital Partners. They plan more than 25 billion dollars across 25 gigawatts of power projects, mostly in the US.

[Pause]

Now the brains. The Advanced Technology Research Council, or ATRC, was set up in 2020 and is chaired by Sheikh Khaled. By February 2024, it had 10 research centres. They held [Pause] 851 researchers.

Its applied research arm, the Technology Innovation Institute, or TII, built Falcon. The Abu Dhabi Media Office calls it the UAE's first generative AI large language model. That's a model trained on huge amounts of text to understand and write language.

[Pause]

In November 2023, ATRC launched AI71 to sell Falcon to businesses. Clients can host it themselves and keep control of their data.

[On-Screen Text: MBZUAI · established 2019 · Masdar City]

Then there's MBZUAI, set up in 2019 in Masdar City. It calls itself the world's first graduate-level, research-based AI university. Since February 2025, Khaldoon Al Mubarak has chaired its board.

[Pause]

Here's the hardest idea: how it all interlocks. Think of a family-run airline group. The board, the AIATC, decides strategy. The treasury, Mubadala and MGX, buys stakes in airlines and plane makers abroad. The operating airline is G42. Through Core42, Khazna and Presight, it flies the routes. [Pause] The flight school is MBZUAI. The engineering workshop is TII. They train the pilots and design the parts.

[Section: Dubai's lane]

So where is Dubai? It runs a different lane.

In June 2023, Sheikh Hamdan bin Mohammed, Crown Prince of Dubai, launched the Dubai Centre for Artificial Intelligence, under the Dubai Future Foundation. It aimed to train [Pause] 1,000 government employees in generative AI.

[On-Screen Text: Dubai Universal Blueprint for AI · spring 2024 · D33 agenda]

In spring 2024 came the Dubai Universal Blueprint for AI, a yearly plan tied to Dubai's D33 economic agenda. That agenda targets [Pause] 100 billion dirhams a year from the digital economy.

[Pause]

Phase one puts a Chief AI Officer in every Dubai government entity. It also brings incubators for AI, and for Web3, meaning blockchain-based services. There's a Dubai commercial licence for AI, and fast-tracked land for data centres.

Read the sources side by side, and a division of labour appears. Dubai focuses on using AI in government and attracting companies. Abu Dhabi's institutions hold the compute, the capital and the research.

[Section: The American bargain]

Finally, the deal that ties all this to the United States.

[On-Screen Text: Microsoft → G42 · $1.5bn · 16 April 2024]

In April 2024, Microsoft announced a [Pause] 1.5 billion dollar investment in G42. Brad Smith joined G42's board, and G42 agreed to run its AI on Microsoft's Azure cloud.

The deal sits under an Intergovernmental Assurance Agreement with both governments. Microsoft says it covers cybersecurity, export controls, responsible AI and "know your customer", meaning checking who actually uses the computers.

[Pause]

There was a condition behind it. Bloomberg reports that G42 had promised to divest from Huawei, which cleared the way.

Microsoft says it will invest [Pause] 15.2 billion dollars in the UAE. That runs from 2023 to 2029, and includes the G42 stake.

[On-Screen Text: Microsoft: 21,500 + 60,400 A100-equivalents · G42: 35,000 GB300-equivalents]

The real bottleneck is chips. A GPU is the processor that does AI's heavy maths, and exporting the best ones needs a US licence. Microsoft says Biden-era licences brought in the equivalent of 21,500 Nvidia A100s. [Pause] Licences approved in September 2025 add the equivalent of 60,400 more.

Then, in November 2025, Bloomberg reported a first. The Commerce Department approved chip sales to G42 itself, equal to 35,000 of Nvidia's GB300 processors. It came with "rigorous security and reporting requirements".

[Pause]

[On-Screen Text: UAE-US AI Campus · 5GW · Stargate UAE · 1GW · first 200MW]

In May 2025, a 5-gigawatt UAE-US AI Campus was unveiled in Abu Dhabi. G42 builds it. Its compute is reserved for US hyperscalers, the giant cloud companies, and approved providers. The White House says the UAE committed to build or finance US data centres at least as large.

Inside it sits Stargate UAE, a 1-gigawatt cluster. OpenAI and Oracle operate it, and NVIDIA supplies the chips. The partners expected the first 200 megawatts in 2026. Reuters puts the first phase at [Pause] 30 billion dollars.

[Pause]

The plan is moving. In September 2026, Reuters reported the campus would likely become a network of sites, after the Iran war. It cited six people familiar with the plans. G42 said work is "progressing as planned" and "subject to continuous review."

[On-Screen Text: Wissam's view]

Now, this part is my own view, not a reported fact. [DRAFT VIEW: founder to confirm]

After 25 years selling enterprise software in this region, I've learned one thing. To understand a market, find out who signs. [Pause] In the UAE, the same handful of people sign at the council, the fund, the builder and the university. I think that is why things move so fast here. A strategy becomes a company, a fund and a data centre in months, not years.

But concentration cuts both ways. The American bargain shows it. Chips arrived with conditions attached, and supplier choices were part of the price. For any leader partnering here, the question isn't just "what does this company sell?" It's "which part of the system am I really dealing with?"

[Section: The bottom line]

Three things to remember.

One: the rules come from two layers. There's the federal AI minister and Strategy 2031, and Abu Dhabi's AIATC, where the money and machines sit.

[Pause]

Two: a few people connect everything. Tahnoon, Khaldoon Al Mubarak and Peng Xiao keep reappearing across the council, G42 and MGX. Dubai runs a separate lane focused on using AI.

[Pause]

Three: the whole system is tied to Washington. Chips, campuses and investment come with US security conditions, and the campus plan itself remains under review.
